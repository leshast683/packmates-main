Spain Packing FAQ

What should I pack for northern Spain?

Layers and a rain jacket, whatever the month. The north — Galicia, the Basque Country, Asturias — gets Atlantic weather: green because it rains. Even in summer, pack a mid-layer and waterproof shoes alongside your warm-weather clothes. It's a different list from the south, and trips that cross both need both halves.

How hot does Spain get in summer, and what should I wear?

The south and centre get properly hot and dry — light, breathable clothes, a hat, sunscreen and a refillable bottle are the core list, and long sightseeing days are best split around the midday heat, when many smaller shops close for a few hours. Save your walking for mornings and evenings, like everyone there does.

What should I pack for evenings in Spain?

One smart-casual outfit and a light layer. Dinner starts late — 9 or 10 pm is normal — and evenings are when Spanish cities come alive, so you'll be out later and walking home later than at home. Neat, comfortable clothes are the standard; nobody expects formal wear.

How do I keep my things safe in Spanish cities?

Use a crossbody bag worn in front in crowds and on the metro, keep your phone off café tables at the edge, and carry a photo or copy of your passport rather than the original where that's practical. Pickpocketing in the big tourist centres is the one real risk to plan for — a bag that zips and stays in front of you handles most of it.

Can one packing list cover both beach and city stops in Spain?

Yes, if you build it from the stops rather than the country. Beach kit plus walking shoes, an evening outfit and one layer covers most mixed trips; add the rain jacket if the route goes north.
