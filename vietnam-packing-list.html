🧳 Pack Like a Local

1

A poncho rides in the day bag over a jacket, because sudden downpours are the local rhythm and ponchos are cheap and sold everywhere if you forget one. A dry bag protects the phone on boat days, and a light layer travels for the aggressively air-conditioned buses and trains — a genuinely local trick that first-time visitors always miss.

Vietnam Packing FAQ

How do I pack for both north and south Vietnam in one trip?

Pack layers, not two wardrobes. In the cooler months Hanoi can need a jacket and long trousers while Ho Chi Minh City is hot — the same trip. A light warm layer, worn or packed on top, plus your normal hot-weather clothes covers both. Build the list stop by stop: in Packmates AI, each destination adds its own weather, so the Hanoi layer survives even though the trip ends at the beach.

What should I pack for Vietnam's rainy season?

A light waterproof jacket or poncho, quick-dry clothes, and a dry bag for your phone on boat days. Rain usually comes as heavy bursts rather than all-day drizzle, and ponchos are sold cheaply everywhere — many travellers buy one there. Shoes you don't mind soaking, plus sandals, beat one precious pair of trainers.

What do I need for motorbike days in Vietnam?

Closed shoes, long trousers and a small bag that stays on your back — you'll likely ride at least once, as a passenger on a tour or between sights. Tours provide helmets. Sun and wind add up over a day on a bike, so sunscreen and a light long-sleeved layer earn their space.

Is there a dress code for temples in Vietnam?

Yes — covered shoulders and knees for pagodas and temples, and shoes off at the door in many places. A light scarf or cover-up in your day bag handles it, same as elsewhere in the region. Slip-on shoes make the shoes-off stops easier.

What is the one thing people forget for Vietnam?

A warm layer for the transport. Buses and trains run their air-conditioning hard, and an overnight journey in shorts is miserable. A light fleece or hoodie in your day bag fixes it — and doubles as your Hanoi layer in the cooler months.
