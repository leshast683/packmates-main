South Korea Packing FAQ

What should I pack for South Korea in winter?

A real winter kit. Seoul winters are dry but properly cold, often below freezing with wind on top: a warm coat, thermal layers, hat, gloves and warm walking shoes. The good news is that indoors is heated strongly — floors included — so build the warmth in layers you can take off inside rather than one heavy outfit.

When is South Korea's rainy season, and what do I pack for it?

The summer monsoon (jangma) runs roughly from late June through July, on top of hot, humid weather. Pack a compact umbrella or light rain jacket, quick-dry clothes and shoes that can get wet. Rain comes in heavy spells rather than all-day drizzle, and the rest of summer is about heat and humidity, so breathable fabrics do double duty.

Do I need special shoes for South Korea?

Slip-on, comfortable walking shoes — you'll take your shoes off often: temples, traditional restaurants and older establishments have shoe-off floors. Shoes you can get in and out of fast, plus socks in good condition, are the local standard. You'll also walk a lot between subway stations and sights, so comfort comes before style.

How much space should I leave for shopping in Seoul?

More than you think — skincare and fashion shopping is a highlight for many visitors, and bags fill up fast. Pack a foldable extra bag inside your suitcase, or arrive with your case a third empty. In Packmates AI you can add "shopping" as an activity on the Seoul days, which is a decent reminder to leave that space on purpose rather than by luck.

What should I pack for cherry blossom season in South Korea?

Layers for changeable spring weather: mild days, cool evenings, and the odd rain shower. Spring also brings fine-dust days, when locals wear masks outdoors — packing a couple is normal, not excessive. Comfortable shoes for blossom parks and palace grounds complete a light list; it's the easiest season to pack for after autumn.
