🧳 Pack Like a Local

Locals pack one pair of flat shoes with real grip and never bring heels near a ruin — marble and worn stone are slippery, and heels are actually banned at sites like the Acropolis for the stone's protection. A light scarf or sarong lives in the day bag at all times, ready to become a church cover-up, a beach wrap, or a layer against the wind on a ferry deck.

Water shoes are the other local habit — Greek beaches are often rocky or volcanic, not soft sand, and bare feet don't last long on them.

🚫 What Not to Overpack

Heels. Banned at major archaeological sites and genuinely useless on stepped, stone-paved island lanes.

A suitcase you can't carry. Island lanes are stairs and cobbles — wheels stop helping fast.

More than one evening outfit. Island evenings are relaxed enough that nobody notices a repeat.

Greece Packing FAQ

What shoes should I pack for Greece?

Flat, broken-in shoes with grip — and leave the heels at home. High heels are actually prohibited at archaeological sites like the Acropolis because they damage the ancient stone, and marble and worn steps are slippery even in flats. Add sandals for the beach and water shoes if you'll swim off rocky or volcanic beaches.

Do I need to cover up for churches and monasteries in Greece?

Yes — covered shoulders and knees are the standard, and stricter monasteries enforce it. Some provide wrap skirts at the entrance, but you can't count on it. A light scarf or sarong in your day bag solves it everywhere, and doubles as a beach cover-up and a ferry layer.

How do I pack for island hopping in Greece?

Pack in a bag you can carry yourself, up stairs and onto ferries — island lanes are stepped, steep and often car-free. Keep a small day bag for the beach-and-ruins days, and a light layer for windy ferry decks, even in July. One list for the whole route works: the islands share a climate, so it's the logistics, not the weather, that change.

What should I pack for Greece in summer?

Light, breathable clothes, serious sun protection (hat, sunscreen, sunglasses), swim kit and a refillable bottle — ancient sites have almost no shade and days are hot and dry. Add the cover-up for churches, flat shoes for sites, and one light layer for breezy evenings and ferries. In Packmates AI, the list is sized by your island stops and dates, so boat days and site days both get covered.

Is one evening outfit enough for Greece?

Yes. Island evenings are relaxed — one smart-casual outfit, worn more than once, covers tavernas and bars everywhere. The space you save is better spent on a second pair of comfortable day shoes, because you'll walk far more than you'll dress up.
