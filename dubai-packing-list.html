Dubai Packing List: What to Actually Pack

Dubai is dry and intensely hot outdoors but heavily air-conditioned indoors — pack for both, plus one modest outfit for mosques and traditional areas.

Dubai is more conservative than its skyline suggests: shoulders and knees covered is expected in malls, and required at mosques. Dubai Packing FAQ

What should I wear in Dubai malls and public places?

Modest, covered clothing is the norm: shoulders and knees covered in malls and public spaces keeps you comfortable and within local expectations. Light, breathable fabrics make that easy in the heat. Swimwear is for the beach and pool, not the mall on the way back.

What do I need to wear to visit a mosque in Dubai?

Mosque dress requirements are strict: women need to cover their hair, arms and legs, and men should cover shoulders and legs too. Bring a light scarf in your day bag so you're ready without reorganising your outfit. It's the one dress rule in Dubai with no flexibility, so plan for it rather than hoping to sort it out at the door.

Why do I need a jacket in Dubai if it's so hot?

Because indoors is a different climate. Malls, restaurants and the Metro run their air conditioning cold, and the jump from desert heat to icy interiors catches everyone out. A light layer that lives in your bag is one of the most-used items on a Dubai trip.

What should I pack for a desert safari in Dubai?

Closed shoes for walking on sand, sunglasses for the daytime glare, and a light jacket — the desert cools down quickly in the evening, which surprises people dressed for the city heat. Loose, comfortable clothes work best for the ride itself. Leave anything precious behind; sand gets everywhere.

What should I pack for Dubai in summer?

Breathable, modest clothing for extreme outdoor heat, planned around short hops between air-conditioned spaces, plus that ever-present light layer for indoors.
